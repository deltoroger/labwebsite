Gerardo studied Biology at the School of Sciences at UNAM in Mexico City. He then pursued his MSc and PhD at CINVESTAV in Irapuato, Mexico, under the supervision of Stewart Gillmor. His doctoral research was recognized with the 2017 Weizmann Prize from the Mexican Academy of Sciences. In 2018, he was awarded an EMBO Postdoctoral Fellowship to join the laboratory of Claudia Köhler at the Swedish University of Agricultural Sciences (SLU). In 2021, he moved to Germany to continue his postdoctoral research at the Max Planck Institute of Molecular Plant Physiology. 

Throughout his scientific career, Gerardo has focused on understanding the genetic and epigenetic mechanisms regulating seed development.
Early in his career, he investigated zygotic genome activation in _Arabidopsis thaliana_, providing functional evidence for the maternal genome's role in controlling early embryogenesis. He subsequently explored the molecular mechanisms underlying hybridization barriers in the endosperm. Using transcriptomic approaches targeting endosperm-specific nuclei, he investigated genomic imprinting, an epigenetic phenomenon characterized by the parent-of-origin (monoallelic) expression of genes involved in seed development.

His research later focused on the relationship between transposable elements and genomic imprinting. By inducing transposon mobilization in Arabidopsis and adapting CUT&Tag to profile histone modifications in the endosperm, he investigated how transposable element activity influences epigenetic regulation of imprinted genes. More recently, his interests have expanded to post-transcriptional regulation and molecular communication between seed compartments.

In 2026, Gerardo joined the Institute of Biotechnology (IBt) at UNAM as a group leader, where he established the Genomics and Developmental Trajectories Laboratory.

Outside the lab, Gerardo enjoys martial arts, watching movies, and, apparently, is quite a good salsa dancer.
